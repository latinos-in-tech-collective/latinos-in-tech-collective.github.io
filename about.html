Since 2016

The community we wished existed.

Latinos in Tech started as six people splitting a pupusa after work, comparing notes on being the only one in the room. It's grown into a few thousand people who still, mostly, act like that.

None of us set out to start an organization. A few engineers in the Bay Area were tired of Slack channels that went quiet after the initial "welcome" and conferences where the only other Latino in the room was working the registration desk. So they started meeting up. Then they started inviting people. By the second year, the meetups needed a mailing list. By the fourth, they needed a name.

We're still, at heart, that same instinct: find the other person in the room, and make the next room bigger. What's changed is the infrastructure — mentorship pairings instead of hallway introductions, chapters instead of one group chat, a real budget instead of everyone chipping in for coffee.

How we got here

2016

Six people, one group chat

What started as informal meetups in the Boston turns into a recurring monthly dinner.

2018

First chapter outside the Bay

A cluster of members in Austin ask to start their own chapter. We write down a process instead of winging it every time.

2021

Mentorship program launches

Structured, twelve-week pairings replace the ad hoc "DM me if you need advice" approach.

2024

22 chapters, one Slack that actually gets used

Growth stopped being the goal around here. Showing up for each other stayed the point.
