From bootcamp to tech lead: Marisol's path

By Marisol Contreras, October 2, 2026

I finished my coding bootcamp in a two-bedroom apartment I shared with three other people, on a laptop I'd bought refurbished because the new ones were out of budget. I mention this because every article about "breaking into tech" seems to skip the part where it's actually hard, and I promised myself I wouldn't do that if I ever got the chance to write one.

My first job was a contract QA role that had nothing to do with what I'd trained for. I took it because rent doesn't wait for the right opportunity. Six months in, the contract ended and wasn't renewed. That was layoff number one, though it didn't feel dramatic enough at the time to call it that.

I found Latinos in Tech through a friend of a friend's Instagram story, of all things. I almost didn't apply to the mentorship program — I remember thinking I didn't have enough of a "story" yet to be worth mentoring. I was wrong about that, and I'm glad someone talked me out of the thought.

That reframe mattered more than any interview tip. I stopped treating the contract role and the gap after it like something to explain away, and started talking about what I'd actually learned from both — mostly how to keep functioning when a job disappears out from under you with two weeks' notice.

The second layoff came eighteen months later, at a startup that ran out of runway faster than anyone expected. By then I had a mentor who'd been through it herself, a group chat of people going through the same thing in real time, and enough perspective to know it wasn't a referendum on whether I belonged in this industry.

I lead a team of four now. Two of them are former mentees of mine — one from this same program. I still don't think of myself as someone with a tidy career story. But I've stopped needing one to be worth listening to, and that's mostly what I'd want someone reading this, mid-gap, to take from it.
